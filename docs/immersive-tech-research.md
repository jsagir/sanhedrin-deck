## 1. Holographic / Projection Technology

### Pepper's Ghost (Still the Workhorse)
The classic 45-degree angled glass/film illusion remains the most reliable "hologram" for museums due to its brightness, viewing angle, and zero-glasses requirement.

- **Musion (UK)** - The gold standard. Created the Tupac Coachella hologram. Their Musion EyeLiner system uses a proprietary foil with 90%+ transparency. Works at massive scale (stage-sized) or in cabinet form. Used in museums globally. URL: musion.com
- **HYPERVSN (UK/Ukraine)** - LED fan-based "holographic" displays. Their SmartV Wall can tile units into large-format floating images. Not true holography but extremely eye-catching for lobbies and entryways. Good for logos/simple animations, less ideal for realistic human figures. URL: hypervsn.com
- **Holobox by Dreamoc / Realfiction (Denmark)** - Self-contained Pepper's ghost cabinets in various sizes (Holobox, Holobox Mini). The Dreamoc range goes from tabletop to human-scale. Perfect for displaying a single "character in a box." Their DeepFrame product is a transparent screen alternative. URL: realfiction.com
- **Proto Inc. (US)** - The "Proto M" and "Proto Epic" are self-contained holographic communication boxes. Originally for live holographic calls but increasingly used for pre-recorded AI characters. The Epic is human-scale (7ft). Key advantage: self-contained, plug-and-play, no darkroom needed. URL: protohologram.com
  - **PROVEN IN MUSEUMS (2025-2026)**: Crystal Bridges Museum (Bentonville, AR) deployed a Proto Epic for Jewel's exhibition where she appears as a hologram explaining show themes and performing a song written for the exhibit. The Walmart Museum uses Proto + StoryFile AI to let visitors ask questions to a hologram of Sam Walton. The Theodore Roosevelt Inaugural National Historic Site deployed "Holo-Ted" - a conversational AI avatar of the 26th President where visitors ask real-time questions and get contextual responses.
- **RAVATAR (International)** - PURPOSE-BUILT for exactly the AI hologram museum use case. Combines real-time AI avatars with holographic displays. URL: ravatar.com
  - **Elliott Museum (Florida, 2025)**: Deployed an AI hologram of Howard Carter (Tutankhamun's discoverer) in a holobox. Visitors engage in real-time dialogue, Q&A, and storytelling with Carter's avatar.
  - **VivaTech 2025 (Paris)**: Leonardo da Vinci appeared as a real-time AI hologram at Capgemini's booth - projected inside a holobox, taking live questions from attendees.
  - **Abu Dhabi (2025)**: Bilingual AI government assistant deployed on stage at a public-sector summit.
  - RAVATAR's 2025 platform features improved lipsync, stronger motion logic, full-body animation, and configurable AI behavior that reacts in the moment rather than scripted responses.
- **ARHT Media (Canada)** - "HoloPresence" capsules. Used for live holographic presentations at conferences. Their tech can beam a live person or AI avatar into a display unit. Already in museum/educational contexts. URL: arhtmedia.com
- **HoloTheater by BASE Xperiential + MacGillivray Freeman Films (US, NEW 2025)**
  - First custom-built holographic theater in the US, opened November 2025 at the Museum of Discovery and Science (MODS) in Fort Lauderdale.
  - Launched with "Dinosaur Discoveries: A Holographic Adventure" - a 24-minute glasses-free experience.
  - Next production: "Ocean Giants: A Holographic Odyssey" slated for 2026.
  - Business model: museum converted an underutilized space into a revenue-generating experience.
  - This is the closest existing format to a "Sanhedrin debate theater" - a purpose-built room for holographic content with audience seating.

### Best Options for Dynamic AI Character Switching (< 2 seconds)

For an AI-powered "Sanhedrin" debate where characters switch dynamically:

1. **LED Wall/Screen + Real-time AI Avatar** (Recommended Primary)
   - A high-resolution LED wall (or multiple) with AI-generated characters rendered in real-time. Character switching is instant (just change the video feed). Modern fine-pitch LED (P1.2 or smaller) from Leyard, Unilumin, or Samsung "The Wall" gives cinema-quality at close range.
   - Pair with: directional speakers per character position, DMX-controlled spot lighting.

2. **Multiple Proto Hologram Units** (Premium "WOW")
   - Place 3-7 Proto Epic units in a semicircle (the "Sanhedrin" arrangement). Each unit shows one character. Characters can switch instantly because each is just a video feed. The 3D floating-head effect is dramatic and theatrical.
   - Cost: ~$25,000-65,000 per unit depending on size.
   - Advantage: Each character has physical spatial presence. Audience can walk around them.

3. **Pepper's Ghost Stage** (Theatrical Maximum)
   - A curved Pepper's ghost setup with multiple projection zones. Characters appear to stand on a physical stage. Content switching is instant. Can layer characters at different depths.
   - Requires: dedicated dark space, careful lighting design, professional installation.
   - Suppliers: Musion, Holosonics, or custom fabrication.

4. **Transparent OLED Array** (Modern/Clean)
   - Samsung or LG transparent OLED panels arranged as "windows" into character spaces. Physical set pieces behind the glass create depth. Characters rendered on the transparent panel appear to occupy the physical space behind.
   - Advantage: works in ambient light, no special darkroom needed.

## 2. AI Talking Portraits / Digital Humans

### Current State of the Art (Real-time Lip-sync + TTS)

  - September 2025: D-ID acquired simpleshow (global explainer video pioneer), expanding their content creation pipeline.

- **HeyGen (US)** - Interactive Avatar product launched 2024. Can do real-time conversation with a digital human. Their "Streaming Avatar" API is designed for kiosk/embed use. Quality is among the best. Latency ~1.5-3 seconds. URL: heygen.com
  - Their "Instant Avatar" can clone a person from 2 minutes of video.

  - Advantage: integrates directly with Azure OpenAI, so the whole pipeline (LLM reasoning + TTS + avatar) is on one platform.
  - Supports Hebrew TTS via Azure Neural Voices.

- **Synthesia (UK)** - The leading AI video platform. Their "Expressive Avatars" (launched late 2024) add emotional range. Real-time interactive mode is newer but improving. Primary strength is pre-rendered content, but their API supports near-real-time for interactive use. URL: synthesia.io

- **Soul Machines (New Zealand)** - WAS purpose-built for interactive digital humans. Their "Digital People" platform was designed for kiosk/museum/retail with full facial animation, emotional response, and real-time conversation. URL: soulmachines.com

**Tier 2: Open Source / Self-Hosted (Lower Latency Possible)**

- **SadTalker** - Open source talking head generation. Can run on local GPU for lowest possible latency. Quality is good but below commercial options. GitHub: OpenTalker/SadTalker
- **Wav2Lip** - Lip-sync any audio to any face video. Very fast, can run real-time on GPU. Lower visual quality than commercial options but the fastest option for character switching.
- **LivePortrait (Kuaishou/GitHub)** - Released mid-2024, became hugely popular. Animates a portrait photo with expression transfer. Fast enough for near-real-time on modern GPU. GitHub: KwaiVGI/LivePortrait
- **MuseTalk (TMElyralab)** - Real-time lip sync, runs at 30fps+ on consumer GPU. Quality approaching commercial. GitHub: TMElyralab/MuseTalk

- **NVIDIA Audio2Face / ACE** - NVIDIA's Avatar Cloud Engine. Designed for game characters but applicable to museum digital humans. Real-time facial animation from audio. The "James" demo showed photorealistic real-time conversation. URL: nvidia.com/en-us/ai/ace

**For < 2 Second Character Switching, the Best Architecture Is:**

Run multiple character renderers in parallel (one per character), each with its own pre-loaded portrait/model. When a character "speaks," route TTS audio to that character's renderer and switch the display feed. This avoids cold-start latency entirely.

Recommended stack:
- **LLM**: Claude or GPT-4o for debate generation (character persona prompts)
- **TTS**: ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 - **75ms latency, 135ms time-to-first-byte** (best in field). 5-second voice cloning in 30+ languages. Their Eleven v3 model adds maximum expressiveness and emotional range for creative applications. URL: elevenlabs.io
- **Lip-sync/Avatar**: D-ID V4 Expressive Visual Agents (sub-0.5s, 4K) or self-hosted MuseTalk/Linly-Talker-Stream on local GPU
- **Display**: LED wall segments, Proto units, or Looking Glass HLD per character

## 3. Immersive Audience Engagement

### Handheld/Personal Devices

- **Audience Smartphones (Lowest Friction)**
  - Tools: **Slido** (by Cisco), **Mentimeter**, **Poll Everywhere**, **Kahoot!**
  - QR code on entry, instant browser-based participation. No app download needed.
  - Can do: live voting, word clouds, Q&A, sentiment sliders, multiple choice.
  - Slido specifically supports "debate mode" polling with real-time visualization.

- **Custom Tablet Stations**
  - Mounted iPads or Android tablets at each seat or station.
  - More controlled experience than BYOD.
  - Can show character bios, voting interfaces, argument summaries.

- **NFC/RFID Wristbands**
  - Issue on entry. Visitors tap to "vote," "align with a character," or "challenge a point."
  - Creates a physical gesture that adds drama (standing up and walking to a voting station).
  - Suppliers: Intellitix, Glownet, RFID4U.

### Audience Response Systems (Purpose-Built)

- **IML Audience Response** - Professional audience voting systems with physical handsets. Used in TV shows (Who Wants to Be a Millionaire style). LED handset shows your vote. URL: iml.co.uk
- **CLiKAPAD** - Dedicated audience response clickers. Simple, reliable, no WiFi needed. URL: clikapad.com
- **Turning Technologies** - ResponseCards and LCD keypads for audience voting. Used in education and events. URL: turningtechnologies.com

### Live Sentiment Visualization (This Creates DRAMA)

- **Real-time Vote Walls**: Project audience votes onto a large secondary display. As people vote, they see the room's opinion shift in real-time. Use animated bar charts, heat maps, or "tug of war" visualizations.
- **Sentiment Heatmaps**: If using mobile devices, track which arguments are resonating. Show a "conviction meter" that rises and falls during the debate.
- **Physical Voting**: Have audience members physically move to different sides of the room to show allegiance (like the UK Parliament division lobbies). Combine with overhead camera + projection to show the "balance of opinion."
- **"Audience Mood Ring"**: LED strip lighting around the room that shifts color based on aggregate audience sentiment (blue = calm/analytical, red = passionate/disagreeing, green = consensus). Creates ambient emotional feedback.

### Gamification Approaches

- **Argument Cards**: Issue physical cards at entry representing different values/priorities. Audience plays cards to influence which arguments characters make next.
- **Conviction Points**: Audience earns points for engaging (voting, asking questions). Top participants get recognized on a leaderboard - or get to "summon" an additional character.
- **Alliance System**: Audience members align with different historical figures. The "faction" with the most support influences the debate direction.
- **Post-Experience Takeaway**: Print a personalized "Your Civic Profile" card at the exit based on how they voted during the experience. This is the souvenir they take home and share on social media.

### What Creates "Drama" in a Museum Context

1. **Conflict and Stakes**: The characters must DISAGREE. Polite agreement is boring. The AI characters should have genuine philosophical tensions.
2. **Audience Agency**: When the audience realizes their votes ACTUALLY CHANGE what happens next, engagement skyrockets. This is the magic moment.
3. **Surprise/Reveal**: A character that was losing the debate suddenly makes a devastating argument. The lights change. The music shifts. A new character appears unexpectedly.
4. **Emotional Escalation**: Start calm and intellectual, build to passionate disagreement, resolve with unexpected common ground.
5. **Physical Spectacle**: The room itself should respond - lights, sound, projections on walls shifting with the debate's emotional arc.

## 4. Spatial Audio & Lighting

### Directional / Spatial Audio

- **Holosonics Audio Spotlight (US)** - Ultrasonic directional speaker that creates a tight beam of sound. Stand in the beam, you hear it clearly. Step out, silence. Perfect for giving each character its own "voice zone." URL: holosonics.com
  - Used in museums worldwide (Smithsonian, Holocaust Memorial Museum, etc.)
  - 2-3 foot beam width at typical distances.

- **Audfly (China)** - Indoor directional speakers. Nintendo adopted Audfly's speakers for their museum project in 2024, creating unique audio experience zones for each classic game exhibit. Each display had its own dedicated audio zone without disturbing other displays. URL: audflyspeaker.com
  - Another example of China leading in affordable museum-grade hardware.

- **Focusonics (UK)** - Parametric speakers similar to Holosonics. Model C and Model A for different applications. URL: focusonics.com

- **L-Acoustics L-ISA (France)** - Immersive spatial audio processing. Places sound objects in 3D space using a speaker array. Used in concerts (Adele, Billie Eilish) and installations. Characters' voices could move through the room. URL: l-acoustics.com/l-isa

- **d&b Soundscape (Germany)** - Object-based spatial audio. Their "En-Scene" and "En-Space" processing can place sound sources precisely in a room. Used in theaters and museums. URL: dbaudio.com/en/solutions/soundscape

- **Bose Professional (US)** - Their "ShowMatch" and spatial audio systems are popular in museum installations. Bose also makes directional speakers for exhibit cases. URL: pro.bose.com

- **Apple Spatial Audio / Dolby Atmos for Headphones** - If using personal devices (AR glasses, headphones), spatial audio is built into iOS/AirPods. Each character could have a distinct position in the listener's 3D soundfield.

### DMX/ArtNet Lighting Integration

- **ETC (Electronic Theatre Controls)** - Industry standard for theatrical lighting control. Their "Eos" family of consoles and "ColorSource" LED fixtures are widely used in museums. URL: etcconnect.com
  - ETC Eos can be triggered by external software via OSC (Open Sound Control) protocol.

- **MA Lighting (grandMA)** - The other industry standard. grandMA3 consoles control complex shows. Can be triggered by external systems via DMX, ArtNet, sACN, or OSC. URL: malighting.com

- **Pharos Controls (UK)** - Specifically designed for architectural and museum lighting. Their controllers respond to triggers (button press, sensor, software command). Perfect for "when character X speaks, spotlight X activates." URL: pharoscontrols.com

- **ArtNet Protocol** - Open protocol that sends DMX over Ethernet. Any software can control lights via ArtNet. Node hardware from Enttec, Sunlite, or DMXking converts ArtNet to physical DMX. Python libraries (like `python-artnet` or `OLA`) make integration straightforward.

- **QLab (Figure 53, US)** - Show control software for Mac. Integrates audio, video, lighting (via OSC/MSC), and projection. Used in theaters and museums. Can be triggered by external events. Perfect for orchestrating the whole show. URL: figure53.com

### Dynamic Spotlight / Tracking Systems

- **Follow-Me (Netherlands)** - Automated follow-spot system. Uses tracking to keep a spot on a moving target. Can be adapted to highlight whichever display/character is currently "speaking." URL: follow-me.nl
- **ZACTRACK (Austria)** - Real-time tracking system for stage. Tracks performers/objects and controls lights, sound, and video to follow them. URL: zactrack.com
- **Robert Juliat (France)** - Premium followspot and profile fixtures. Their automated fixtures can be pre-programmed to snap between positions (= character positions). URL: robertjuliat.com
- **Martin by Harman MAC Series** - Moving head fixtures that can snap to preset positions in <0.5 seconds. Program each character position as a preset, trigger via DMX/ArtNet when that character speaks.

### Creating Theatrical Atmosphere

The key is **synchronized state changes**:
- When Character A starts speaking: Spotlight A brightens, others dim. Character A's directional speaker activates. A subtle ambient sound bed shifts to match the character's era/personality. Wall projections subtly shift color.
- When the debate intensifies: Overall lighting warms/brightens. Sound levels increase slightly. Projection effects become more dynamic.
- When the audience votes: A "dramatic pause" - lights dim, sound drops. Then the reveal: lights sweep, results projected, winner's zone brightens.

- **Gaussian Splatting for Characters** - Research from multiple labs (ETH Zurich, INRIA, etc.) is enabling Gaussian Splatting for animatable human characters. By 2027, expect real-time, photorealistic, fully animatable characters from just photos/video of a person. No motion capture stage needed.

### Spatial Computing for Museums

- **Apple Vision Pro (2025 M5 refresh + visionOS 26)**
  - Updated with M5 chip in 2025 for improved performance. visionOS 26 adds generative AI-powered spatial photo scenes and new developer capabilities.
  - Museum apps already exist: **Museas** (spatial art museum featuring Da Vinci, Caravaggio, Van Gogh, Hokusai, Picasso), **Coffey & Sons** (spatial car museum, named PCMag Best App 2025).
  - Could overlay AI characters onto physical gallery spaces.
  - "Shared spatial experiences" where multiple visitors see the same virtual content anchored to the real room.
  - Apple's eye/hand tracking enables gesture-based interaction.
  - Challenge: cost per headset, battery life, hygiene for shared use. Interesting but the Apple Museum of Failure exhibit suggests mainstream adoption is still pending.

- **Meta Quest 3 / 3S** - More affordable ($300-500), decent passthrough AR. Meta's "Mixed Reality" mode blends virtual and real. Could work for:
  - Museum-distributed headsets for premium experiences.
  - AI characters appearing to stand in the physical room.
  - Already used in some location-based entertainment (Meta partnered with several venues).

- **Qualcomm AR Glasses Reference Designs** - Lightweight AR glasses (vs. headsets) are coming. Qualcomm's Snapdragon AR2 platform powers glasses from multiple manufacturers. By 2027, expect sub-$500 AR glasses light enough for 1-2 hour museum visits.

- **Spatial Anchors / World Mesh** - Both Apple and Meta platforms can scan a room and anchor virtual content precisely. This means a museum can design an experience once and have it work perfectly every visit. Characters appear at exact positions, gestures toward physical objects work, etc.

### Other Emerging Tech

- **Haptic Feedback** - Companies like Ultraleap use ultrasonic arrays to create tactile sensations in mid-air. Visitors could "feel" a character's presence or feel a vibration when a vote is cast.
  - Ultraleap STRATOS Inspire: mounted above a display, creates haptic feedback on visitors' hands. URL: ultraleap.com

- **Scent Technology** - Vapor Communications, Scentee, OVR Technology. Emit scents on cue. Could evoke historical periods (ancient Jerusalem = incense + stone + olive). Adds a visceral, memorable sensory dimension.
  - OVR Technology: programmable scent cartridges triggered by software. URL: ovr.technology

- **Responsive Architecture** - Kinetic elements (moving walls, rising platforms, rotating stages) combined with projection. Companies like Kinesys (motion control) enable stages that physically reconfigure.

- **AI-Generated Real-Time Environments** - Using Stable Diffusion / DALL-E style models to generate backgrounds/environments in real-time based on the debate topic. If the debate shifts to economics, the projected background shifts to markets. If it shifts to security, the environment darkens and becomes more austere.

## 7. Recommended "WOW Factor" Architecture for a Civic Discourse Experience

### The Showstopper Setup

**Concept: "The Council Chamber"**

A purpose-designed circular or semicircular room (seats 30-80 visitors):

**Display Layer:**
- 5-7 Proto Epic hologram units arranged in a semicircle, each representing one historical/AI character. Characters appear to float in 3D space. Alternatively: a curved LED wall (Samsung The Wall or Chinese manufacturer) divided into character zones, backed by atmospheric projection on surrounding walls.

**Audio Layer:**
- One Holosonics Audio Spotlight per character position (directional sound so each character has spatial location).
- L-Acoustics or d&b subwoofers for dramatic bass during "moment" transitions.
- Overhead ambient speaker array for spatial atmosphere.

**Lighting Layer:**
- ETC or Martin moving head spots - one per character, snap to position in <0.3s.
- Philips Hue or Astera LED strips around the room for ambient mood shifts.
- All controlled via ArtNet/DMX from the show controller.

**AI Layer:**
- Custom LLM agents (Claude/GPT-4o based) with character personas and debate logic.
- ElevenLabs for voice (cloned historical voices where recordings exist, custom designed voices otherwise).
- D-ID or self-hosted MuseTalk for real-time lip-sync on character portraits.
- All characters pre-loaded and running in parallel for instant switching.

**Audience Interaction Layer:**
- On entry: QR code to browser-based voting app (Slido-style custom build).
- Physical "conviction stones" - smooth stones with NFC chips. Place your stone at a character's station to show support (tactile, dramatic gesture).
- Overhead camera tracking audience stone placement, visualized on a central floor projection showing the "balance of debate."
- Exit: printed personalized "Your Civic Voice" card summarizing your positions.

**Show Control:**
- QLab or Disguise media server orchestrating all layers.
- AI debate engine sends OSC messages to QLab.
- QLab triggers: lighting changes, audio routing, character switching, projection cues, vote opening/closing.

**Drama Arc (20-30 minute experience):**
1. (2 min) Dark room. Single spotlight on one character. Opening provocation.
2. (5 min) Characters introduced one by one (lights snap, audio activates, hologram appears). Each states their position.
3. (3 min) FIRST AUDIENCE VOTE. Lights dim. Voting opens. Results revealed dramatically.
4. (8 min) Characters debate, responding to audience lean. Characters that are "losing" make stronger arguments. Physical energy builds (lights, sound intensity).
5. (2 min) TWIST: A surprise character appears (projected on the ceiling or behind the audience). Reframes the debate.
6. (3 min) FINAL VOTE. Full room lighting effect for the reveal.
7. (2 min) Resolution / reflection. Characters find unexpected common ground. Lights soften. Music resolves.
8. Exit through "reflection corridor" with key quotes projected on walls and personalized cards printing.
